Cooperative Model

The Roadmap

The journey towards making an effective cooperative model that addresses the needs for primary care a reality has six stages.

The following roadmap outlines what is needed at each stage to build the model—your model. The essential work is to establish the core competency, engage the right people, and create the structures that will support the intended outcome. Each stage lays the foundation for the next, gradually turning a shared vision into a durable cooperative primary care model.

One roadmap. A different route in every place.

The six stages provide the general path for building the model. That sequence holds whether the work begins with a community, an employer, a health system, or an entire region.

The specific plan emerges on the ground. It depends on who is ready to lead, what capacity already exists, which resources can be brought together, and what the local environment will allow. Those conditions determine the pace and shape of the work.

The roadmap remains consistent. The route and the timeline belong to the place where your journey ends.

The six stages

The first three stages build the foundation. This is also where efforts like this often die quietly preserved in a business plan that no one seriously pressure tested. The mistake is assuming, “We have seen this before,” instead of asking, “What must we look at differently this time?”

The final three stages turn preparation into build out. This is where the model moves off the page and becomes a functioning clinical resource.

Prepare — where meaning meets mission
Stage One
Build real clinical confidence by defining your core competency
Walk a target patient through the full arc, dozens of times, until the plan breaks and gets rebuilt before anyone real walks in.
Stage Two
Solve the veil of statistics
National numbers were never built to measure what happens between a patient and a care team — this stage builds a measurement plan that can.
Stage Three
Design the fee strategy as real, tested numbers
Membership, copay, cash pool, insurance — priced and pressure-tested at real scale, not sketched as a diagram.
Execute — where preparation becomes a real clinic
Stage Four
Find and sign the first anchor
The payoff of the first three stages: one anchor organization, formally committed, on terms that work for everyone at the table.
Stage Five
Hold the arc — do good work and give it time
Patience without passivity — protecting the relationship through the pressure that early operation always brings.
Stage Six
Build the federated backbone — while seeding what comes next
Shared technology, insurance, credentialing, and advocacy — built once, while the next local cooperative already begins.

The horizon

The institutional and policy conversation is deliberately kept outside the six stages rather than made a stage of its own. It begins only after the model has substance behind it. Each failed effort documented in The Record reached for legislation or a ballot before it had demonstrated the care model in practice, then entered its decisive argument asking the public to trust what did not yet exist.

This framework reaches that conversation last. By then, it should carry a functioning clinical model, a tested fee strategy, committed anchors, meaningful measurement, and a multi-site record of care delivered over time. The request is no longer permission to exist. It is partnership, support, and room to continue what has already been shown to work.

The timing of a specific project belongs to the people and places building it. It does not belong to this page. Every effort in The Record carried a projected date into its decisive moment, and each became more difficult to defend when that date passed without the promised result. The six stages are structured as the clinical arc is structured: around a defined endpoint, not a deadline.

The one date on this page is therefore not a promise about pace. It is the horizon toward which the larger framework has been building since before these stages were written. By 2038, the sixtieth anniversary of the Alma-Ata Declaration, the goal is for this framework to be fully public and actively in use—not as a marginal proposal, but as a demonstrated model of execution.

Everything on this page is the path to reaching that horizon honestly: one completed stage at a time, one functioning site at a time, and one result that can be carried forward into the next.
