The Minnesota Initiative

Participate in the Build

The first build requires more than one kind of expertise.

The Minnesota Initiative is assembling the knowledge, relationships, and institutional participation required to complete the early stages of the roadmap.

We are seeking people and organizations able to contribute:

  • clinical review;
  • patient and population experience;
  • healthcare cost and utilization data;
  • outcome and evaluation design;
  • cooperative formation and governance;
  • financing and actuarial analysis;
  • regulatory knowledge;
  • operating infrastructure;
  • or a potential founding population.

Where Different Participants Fit

Clinicians and Care Organizations

Help pressure-test representative clinical arcs, define delivery requirements, and identify the coordination and referral realities the operating model must address.

Employers and Member Organizations

Contribute population needs, cost experience, purchasing knowledge, and the perspective required to evaluate potential anchor participation.

Cooperative Leaders

Help define formation, governance, member accountability, federation, and the shared infrastructure required to support locally owned clinics.

Policymakers and Public Agencies

Contribute regulatory analysis, public data, policy knowledge, convening capacity, and safeguards that protect the public interest.

Researchers and Evaluators

Help design credible outcome measures, comparison methods, implementation learning, and independent review.

Funders and Capital Partners

Support stage-appropriate development and help distinguish early research and design needs from the capitalization required for eventual operation.

The Invitation

Participation at this stage is not an endorsement of a finished model.

It is an invitation to examine the framework, test the roadmap, contribute relevant expertise, and help determine whether Minnesota has the resources and institutional will to build the model well.

The immediate work is specific:

  1. Complete and review the representative clinical arcs.
  2. Define the outcome framework.
  3. Establish the resource and cost requirements of the clinical model.
  4. Identify potential founding populations.
  5. Assemble the expertise required for financing, governance, and regulatory design.

If your organization has knowledge, responsibility, or a population connected to this work, we invite a conversation.

The Work Before Us

PrimaryCare.Solutions began with a clinical question:

What would primary care look like if better health and quality of life were its operating objectives?

The framework that followed defines the hub, the arc of care, the appropriate use of clinical tools, the validation of progress, and the patient's movement toward greater capacity and agency.

The Minnesota Initiative asks the next question:

Can these principles be organized, financed, governed, and sustained as a community-owned primary care system?

The Minnesota Roadmap is how we intend to answer it.

Build the clinical model. Define the outcomes. Establish the financing. Organize the founding population. Create the infrastructure. Expand without dilution.

Then return what is learned so other communities can build from it.
