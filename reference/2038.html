A Reality by 2038

The Vision

The desire to build a primary care system that is globally accessible, community-powered, and economically sustainable — one that treats quality of life as the foundation of medicine, not an outcome measured after the fact.

Globally accessible
  • Grounded in the Declaration of Alma-Ata (1978): health as a fundamental human right, primary care as the mechanism for achieving it
  • Scientifically sound and socially acceptable — the Declaration's bar for legitimate care is accessible if we decide to do the work and trust in our ability to care
  • No proprietary content — the barrier to entry is doing the work, learning to trust the model as a foundation
  • One model, built to travel — adapted to a place, to people, to community
Community-powered
  • Member-owned cooperatives, rooted in the communities they serve
  • A federated backbone that lets local clinics share real infrastructure — technology, insurance, training — building the resources around the space between patients and their care team
  • Governance that belongs to the community
Economically sustainable
  • Better delivery is the path; better care is the service, better outcomes at lower costs is the outcome
  • The insurance intermediary removed from primary care, replacing a premium with direct care
  • Insurance restored to its proper place — the layer behind catastrophic events, while direct care carries the everyday relationship
Quality of life as the foundation
  • Capacity measured against a person's own potential
  • Full functional recovery as the target endpoint: resolution carried all the way through, past the point where relief alone would have stopped
  • Health care exists only in the relationship between a patient and their care team — everything else is infrastructure in service of that, or it's overhead pretending to be care

The Challenge

None of this waits for someone else to go first.

  • Every reform effort that asked for permission before the proof existed — Vermont, Colorado, California, the UK — lost that argument. This model provides the method, so you can go looking for the conversation that puts it in play.
  • Fifteen years of practice already exist — a working clinic on the way to becoming many.
  • The failure was always in delivery — turning what's already known into consistent care for real people, not a shortage of money or knowledge.
  • The roadmap is written — six stages, real mechanics, building toward a single horizon: this one.
  • 2038 is the horizon this framework has been building toward since 2002, and the only thing left to determine is how many hands help carry it there.
